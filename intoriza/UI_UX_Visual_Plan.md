# UI/UX Visual Plan

## Global Design & Navigation
- **Branding & Color Scheme:** Use PakGusu’s branding (e.g., company logo and colors) consistently. A clean, professional look with a light background (white or light gray) to emphasize cleanliness, complemented by a primary brand color (e.g., blue or green) for highlights and CTAs.
- **Typography:** Clean, easy-to-read fonts (e.g., a sans-serif for headings and body) that convey a modern and technical feel. Headings should be bold and clear; body text should be legible with adequate spacing.
- **Header & Navigation:** A fixed top navigation bar for easy access. The header should include the logo on the left and a clear menu on the right (or a hamburger menu on mobile). On hover, menu items might have a subtle highlight or dropdown for subpages. The top bar may also show contact info (phone/email) as a thin band.
- **Footer:** A consistent footer on all pages with contact details, important links (site map, social media), and perhaps a quick contact CTA. Design it in a contrasting background color for emphasis.

## Home Page
- **Hero Section:** A full-screen banner with a high-quality image or subtle video of a cleanroom facility. Overlay the tagline and a prominent CTA button (“Request a Quote” or “Learn More”) centered. Text and button animate in (e.g., a fade-in or slide-up) as the page loads to draw attention.
- **Layout:** The homepage uses a scrolling layout with distinct sections. Plenty of white space is used to maintain a clean aesthetic. Sections alternate between full-width backgrounds (light grey or brand color accents) and white to create visual separation.
- **Animations & Interaction:** As users scroll, employ moderate scroll-reveal animations (e.g., fade-in for text blocks, slide-in for images) for each section to add a dynamic feel. Product highlight cards might have hover effects (e.g., slight zoom or shadow) indicating interactivity. If a carousel is used for products or testimonials, include arrow controls and possibly auto-rotation with smooth transitions.
- **Responsiveness:** On mobile, the hero image crops appropriately with the tagline still visible. The grid sections (products, services, industries) collapse into vertical stacks or horizontal scrollables. Text remains readable by using a slightly larger base font on small screens. The CTA button in the hero remains prominent (centered and perhaps full-width on mobile).

## About Section & Pages
- **Visual Style:** The About pages (About PakGusu, About GUSU China, Cleanroom Classifications) share a cohesive look. A banner image or header is used for each, for instance a photo of the company facility for PakGusu, a corporate office or team for GUSU China, and an abstract cleanroom graphic for the classifications page.
- **Layout:** Use a two-column layout for text-heavy pages where possible: for example, text on one side and an illustrative image or infographic on the other. In About PakGusu, a timeline graphic could visually represent company history, with interactive year markers that highlight details on hover.
- **Animations:** Keep animations subtle to maintain professionalism. For example, in the timeline section, milestones might fade or slide into view as the user scrolls down. Icons (for mission, vision, certifications) can have a gentle hover effect.
- **Interaction Design:** Ensure that links to external sites (like GUSU China’s site, if any) or downloadable content (e.g., company brochure PDF) are clearly indicated. The subpage teaser links on the About landing page use interactive cards that highlight on hover or show a brief description overlay.
- **Responsiveness:** On mobile, multi-column sections stack to single column. Timeline or infographic elements become swipeable carousels or vertically stacked for easy mobile scrolling. Images resize to full-width, and text is kept in shorter paragraphs for readability.

## Products Pages
- **Products Overview Page:** Presents product categories in a grid with images. Each category card has an image and the product category name overlay. On hover (or tap), cards may show a brief description or a subtle overlay effect (e.g., a slight lift or shadow).
- **Product Detail Pages:** Each product page uses a consistent template. A top banner or header with a product image or graphic, plus the product name as a title. Below, content is arranged in sections (overview, features, etc.) possibly using tabs or accordions for technical specs if content is lengthy.
- **Visual Elements:** High-resolution images of products are used to illustrate features (e.g., close-up of panel surface or door frame). Use lightbox functionality for images – clicking a product image opens a larger view. Key specs or features might be presented with icons (for example, a thermometer icon for thermal insulation, a shield for durability, etc.) to add visual interest.
- **Animations & Interaction:** As users scroll, sections (like feature lists) fade in. If an accordion or tabbed interface is used for specs, it should smoothly expand/collapse with clear icons indicating open/close states. Hover over product images might zoom slightly to show detail. The category grid on the overview page might have a slight hover highlight to indicate clickability.
- **Responsiveness:** The product category grid on the overview page reorganizes to fewer columns on tablet and a single column on mobile (possibly turning into a swipeable carousel of cards on very small screens). On individual product pages, images scale to full width on mobile, and any multi-column text+image sections stack vertically. Tap-friendly design is ensured for tabs or accordions (e.g., using large touch targets).

## Services Pages
- **Services Overview Page:** Features a section for each service with an icon or image. This could be laid out in a horizontal row or a grid. A clean illustration (like blueprint icon for design, construction helmet for construction, etc.) can accompany each service. On hover, each service box may change background color or show a “Learn More” button.
- **Service Detail Pages:** A hero banner for each service with an illustrative image (e.g., engineers planning for the design service, construction site for construction service) and the service title. Content is broken into sections with clear subheadings.
- **Visual Hierarchy:** Important points (like service steps or benefits) could be highlighted using numbered lists or checkmark icons. For example, the planning service page might have a numbered step-by-step graphic for the design process. Use infographics where possible (like a flowchart from design to installation on the overview or detail pages) to visually engage users.
- **Animations:** Similar to products, content sections appear with subtle animations on scroll. Icons on the overview page might animate (e.g., a gentle pop-in) as they come into view. Ensure animations are not too distracting; they should reinforce the content (like drawing attention to each service as the user scrolls).
- **Responsiveness:** The services overview grid collapses into a single column list on mobile, with icons centered above text for each service. On detail pages, images shrink to full width and text remains in easy-to-read blocks. Any flowcharts or process diagrams are made responsive (perhaps by providing a horizontal scroll or a stacked diagram alternative on mobile).

## Sectors Pages
- **Sectors Overview Page:** Likely a visually rich page with images representing each industry. Use a card or banner for each sector with a background image (pharmaceutical lab, hospital room, food processing line, etc.) overlaid by the sector name. Use a uniform style for all sector cards. On hover, overlay a semi-transparent color and a “Explore” link.
- **Sector Detail Pages:** Each sector page has a banner image relevant to that industry for visual context (e.g., pharma manufacturing line, electronic cleanroom). Content is then presented in a few sections explaining needs and solutions.
- **Imagery & Media:** Utilize industry-specific images within the content to keep it relevant (for example, an image of technicians in clean suits for pharmaceutical, or microchips for electronics). These images can be placed alongside text or in full-width sections to break up text.
- **Interaction & Animations:** Sector cards on the overview could have a slight parallax effect or fade-up on scroll to add depth. Within sector pages, if any statistics or facts are given (like “99.99% contamination reduction”), these can be highlighted with large text or even an animated counter. Hover states on any links or buttons remain consistent with the site (e.g., underline or color change).
- **Responsiveness:** The sector overview page’s image cards stack vertically on mobile (perhaps turning into a swipeable carousel to avoid a long scroll). Banner images on detail pages shrink or crop appropriately for smaller screens, still conveying the context. Paragraphs are kept concise for mobile reading, and any side-by-side image/text sections become top-bottom layout.

## Contact Page
- **Layout & Form:** The contact page has a clean layout with a contact form prominently displayed. Form fields are arranged clearly with labels or placeholder text. To the side (or below on mobile), the contact details (address, phone, email) are shown with recognizable icons (map pin, phone handset, envelope).
- **Visual Elements:** Possibly include an embedded Google map showing the office location for visual context. Use a subtle background (like a light grey or a faint pattern) to distinguish the contact section from the rest of the site.
- **Interaction Design:** Form fields highlight when selected, with clear indications for required fields. There should be input validation with friendly error messages (e.g., if email is not formatted correctly). Upon submission, a success message should confirm the form was sent. The submit button should be high-contrast (primary color) to encourage action.
- **Animations:** Minimal animations here – perhaps a slight fade-in for the form on page load or a hover effect on the submit button. The focus is on usability.
- **Responsiveness:** The contact form fields stack vertically on mobile for easy scrolling and tapping. The address and contact info are placed above or below the form on small screens (rather than side-by-side) to ensure nothing becomes too small. The map, if included, scales to fit the mobile screen width or becomes a static image/link to Google Maps to save space.

## CTA Hierarchy
- **Primary CTA:** The primary call-to-action across the site is to **Contact / Request a Quote**. This is emphasized as a consistently styled button (e.g., bright color, prominent placement) used in key sections like the homepage hero and in the navigation bar (e.g., a "Contact Us" button on the top right).
- **Secondary CTAs:** Secondary actions like “Learn More” or “Explore Products” are styled as less prominent buttons or links (e.g., outlined buttons or subtler color) so they are visible but don’t distract from the primary CTA. For example, on the home hero, primary might be "Get a Quote", secondary might be "Our Products".
- **Usage:** Each page is designed to have at least one clear CTA. Product and service pages end with a CTA banner urging contact or consultation. Sector pages invite the reader to discuss industry-specific solutions. These CTAs use persuasive language combined with keywords (e.g., "Contact us for pharmaceutical cleanroom solutions").
- **Consistency:** All CTA buttons have a consistent shape and hover effect (e.g., slight shadow or color darken on hover) to reinforce that they are clickable and important. The hierarchy is maintained by size and color differences: primary CTAs possibly larger or filled with the brand color, secondaries smaller or outlined.
- **Placement:** Key CTAs are placed where users naturally decide next steps: at the end of pages/sections, and also persistently in the header (making it easy to contact from any page). On mobile, a sticky footer or easily accessible menu ensures the contact option is always available without excessive scrolling.

## Responsive Design Notes
- The entire site is built mobile-first, ensuring that content reflows gracefully on smaller devices. Breakpoints are set (for example, at 1200px, 768px, 480px) to adjust layouts.
- **Navigation:** On tablets and mobiles, the menu collapses into a hamburger icon, which opens a full-screen or dropdown menu. Tap targets are enlarged for finger-friendly interaction.
- **Images & Media:** All images use responsive sizing (CSS max-width: 100%) so they scale down on smaller screens. Decorative images might hide on very small screens if they are non-essential, to reduce clutter.
- **Grid to Stack:** Sections that appear side by side on desktop (e.g., text next to image) stack vertically on mobile, with adequate padding between elements. The order of content is arranged to keep the flow logical (for instance, on a two-column section, we ensure that on mobile the more important content appears first).
- **Typography Adjustments:** Font sizes may adjust slightly for smaller screens (e.g., headings become a bit smaller, body text remains readable by perhaps increasing line height).
- **Testing:** The design is tested across common devices (smartphones, tablets, laptops) to verify that no element is too small or misaligned. Particularly, forms and interactive elements are checked on mobile to ensure ease of use. The result is a site that maintains functionality and aesthetics from large desktops down to small smartphones.
